The basics

What is an HMO?

A house in multiple occupation is a home rented to three or more people who aren't from the same household and who share facilities like the kitchen. Instead of one family renting the whole house, each tenant rents their own room.

The HMOs we build are aimed at young professionals. Every room has its own en-suite shower room, the kitchens are shared, and bills are usually included, so tenants pay one simple monthly amount.

6 rooms × c.£1,000= c.£6,000 a month
Small HMO
3–4 tenants. Often allowed without planning permission, unless an Article 4 direction applies.
Large HMO
5 or more tenants. Needs a mandatory council licence, and usually planning permission.
What tenants get
A furnished en-suite room, shared kitchens, bills included and a flexible tenancy.

Why invest

Why HMOs make a great investment.

01

Much higher rental income

Letting by the room produces far more rent than letting the whole house, which is what makes returns of around 25% cash on cash possible.

02

Income from several tenants

With six tenants, one empty room doesn't stop your income. In a single let, one void means no rent at all.

03

Strong tenant demand

High rents in London and other UK cities mean many young professionals rent a room rather than a whole flat. A quality en-suite room close to transport lets quickly.

04

Value you create

Converting a house adds rooms, en-suites and income. Lenders value HMOs on what they earn, so a good conversion can be worth much more than it cost.

05

Cash you can recycle

Once the house is revalued, a refinance can release much of your original cash, ready to fund the next project and grow a portfolio.

06

A real, physical asset

Your money is in bricks and mortar in the UK, an asset you own outright, not a share of someone else's fund.

What it means for you

Three rules to know.

No Article 4Small HMOs are usually allowed

Turning a house into an HMO for 3 to 6 people is normally permitted development. You still need a licence for 5 or more people.

Article 4 in forcePlanning permission needed

Any HMO needs planning permission, even a small one. Allow around 8 to 13 weeks and a planning fee, and the council can refuse.

7 people or moreAlways needs planning

A large HMO is "sui generis", so it needs full planning permission wherever it is, Article 4 or not.

No Article 4 system. Every HMO needs a licence, and some councils also need planning permission, so check with the council.

Northern Ireland

Different planning and licensing rules apply. Check with the council before you buy.

HMO basics

What is an HMO?+

A house in multiple occupation is a property let to three or more people from more than one household who share facilities such as the kitchen. Each tenant rents their own room, usually with an en-suite, which is why HMOs earn more than a single family let.

Do you buy existing HMOs?+

No. We find ordinary houses that are suitable for HMO conversion and convert them. Creating the HMO is what adds the value that lets you refinance and pull most of your cash back out.

Does an HMO need a licence?+

In England, an HMO with five or more tenants from two or more households needs a mandatory licence from the local council, and some councils license smaller HMOs too. We handle the application and make sure the property meets the council's standards.

What is Article 4, and does it matter?+

Normally a family house can become a small HMO without planning permission. Where an Article 4 direction applies, planning permission is needed. Larger HMOs need planning everywhere. We check this before putting forward any property.

Money and fees

What return can I expect?+

Our projects target a cash-on-cash return of circa 25%: your yearly profit after all costs, divided by the cash left in the deal after refinance. That figure already includes our fees. Results depend on the property, interest rates and the refinance valuation, and returns are not guaranteed.

How do your fees work?+

We never take a percentage of your deal. The property and its profits are 100% yours. We charge a 2% acquisition fee on the purchase price and a 2% management fee on the property's valuation after the works, and both are included in the returns we quote.

When we put the building works or the bridging finance out to tender, the quotes we receive include an arrangement fee payable to Lera. The conveyancer, mortgage broker, and letting and management company we introduce may also pay us an arrangement fee. These are already built into every price, figure and illustration we show you, so there are no extra costs on top.

How much money do I need?+

On our recent six-bedroom project, the investor needed around £225,000 to complete the deal, and around £100,000 remained in it after refinance. We'll give you exact figures for each deal before you commit.

How is the purchase financed?+

Usually with a bridging loan covering around 75% of the purchase price plus the build works. Once the house is converted and let, it's revalued and refinanced onto a long-term mortgage, which repays the bridge. We introduce you to brokers who arrange this.

Can I buy through a limited company?+

Yes, many investors do. Whether it suits you depends on your tax position, so speak to a property accountant first. We can work with either structure.

Is my money at risk?+

As with any property investment, your capital is at risk. Values can fall, costs and interest rates can rise, and rooms can sit empty. We reduce the risk with careful deal selection, managed builds and contingency in every budget, but we can't remove it. Take independent financial advice if you're unsure.

Working with us

Around 5–6 months from purchase to a completed, fully let HMO. Finding the right house comes first and depends on the market.

How involved do I need to be?+

As little as you like. You approve the deal and sign off key decisions, and we keep you updated. Most investors never need to visit site, though you're always welcome.

Do I need to live near the property?+

No. We invest where the numbers work and manage everything on the ground.

Who manages the tenants afterwards?+

The property is professionally managed, covering tenants, bills, cleaning and maintenance. You receive the rental income and a monthly statement.
