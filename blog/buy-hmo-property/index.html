How to buy an HMO: a step-by-step guide for UK investors

Thinking of buying an HMO anywhere in the UK? Here's the whole process, from choosing between a ready-made HMO and a conversion to finance, licensing, letting and refinancing.

Lera Real Estate · Updated 31 October 2026 · 4 min read

If you want to buy an HMO, a house in multiple occupation, you're looking at one of the highest-income ways to invest in UK property. A well-run HMO can earn far more rent than the same house let to a single family. But buying one is not like buying a normal buy-to-let. There are more rules, more costs and more decisions to get right.

This guide walks through the whole process of buying an HMO, step by step.

This is general information, not financial advice. The value of property can go down as well as up. Capital at risk.

Step 1: Decide how you'll buy an HMO

There are three main routes:

RouteWhat it meansProsCons
Buy a ready-made HMOBuy a house already let as an HMO, with tenants in placeIncome from day one, a track record to checkHigher price, less room to add value, older standards may need upgrading
Buy a house and convert itBuy a family house and turn it into an HMOAdd value, design it your way, often a better returnBuilding work, planning and licensing risk, months without rent

Most experienced investors prefer to convert, because that's where the extra value is created. See the BRRR strategy explained.

Step 2: Set your budget

To buy an HMO you'll usually need, in cash:

  • a deposit of around 25% of the purchase price
  • stamp duty, including the 5% surcharge on additional properties. See stamp duty for investors.
  • legal, survey, valuation and finance fees
  • the cost of any conversion works, plus a contingency
  • a cash buffer for the unexpected

For a conversion in London, the total cash needed often runs to well over £150,000, though much of it may come back when you refinance. Use our HMO full calculator to work out your own figures.

Step 3: Choose where to buy

Location decides who will rent from you and how much they'll pay. Look for:

  • strong demand for rooms from professionals, key workers or people on Universal Credit
  • good transport links and local employers
  • room rents that work for your numbers
  • a council whose planning and licensing rules allow the HMO you want

Read choosing an area for an HMO for more.

Step 4: Check planning and Article 4 before you offer

This is the step new investors most often get wrong. In many areas, councils have made Article 4 directions that mean even a small HMO needs planning permission. An HMO for 7 or more people always needs planning. Check any address on our free Article 4 map, and read do you need planning permission for an HMO?

Step 5: Find the right property

The best HMO properties have space to create good-sized bedrooms with en-suites, a big enough kitchen, and room to extend into the loft or at the back. A tired house in a good street is often ideal. See how to find the right property for an HMO.

Step 6: Run the numbers properly

Before you make an offer, work out:

  • realistic rent per room, from let rooms nearby, not just adverts
  • all running costs: bills, council tax, insurance, management, maintenance and compliance
  • a cautious end value after works
  • how much you can refinance, and how much cash stays in the deal
  • your cash flow and return on the money left in

Read HMO yields explained.

Step 7: Arrange finance

To buy an HMO that needs work, most investors use a bridging loan, then switch to an HMO mortgage once the property is finished and let. A ready-made HMO can often be bought with an HMO mortgage straight away. A specialist broker is worth their fee.

Decide early whether to buy in your own name or through a limited company. See buying through a limited company.

Step 8: Do your due diligence

Get a full survey, check the title and planning history, and confirm licensing. If you're buying an existing HMO, ask for its licence, planning proof, certificates, tenancy agreements and rent records. Our HMO due diligence checklist lists 40 things to check.

Step 9: Convert to the right standard

If you're converting, design to the council's room size, amenity and fire safety standards from the start. Most modern HMOs have an en-suite in every bedroom. See what an HMO conversion costs and fire safety in HMOs.

Step 10: License, let and manage

Apply for an HMO licence where needed, then let the rooms to well-referenced tenants and set up professional management. Since May 2026, the Renters' Rights Act has changed how tenancies work, so make sure your paperwork is up to date.

Step 11: Refinance

Once the HMO is finished and let, refinance onto a long-term HMO mortgage based on its new value. If the conversion added enough value, much of your original cash can come back out to fund the next property. See how HMOs are valued.
