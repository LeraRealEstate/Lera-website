HMO room sizes and amenity standards: the numbers that matter

The national minimum bedroom sizes, how councils set kitchen and bathroom standards, and why bigger rooms pay.

Lera Real Estate · Updated 31 October 2026 · 3 min read

Since October 2018, every licensed HMO in England has had to meet national minimum bedroom sizes. Councils then add their own standards for kitchens, bathrooms and toilets.

Minimum bedroom sizes

Who sleeps thereMinimum floor area
Two people aged over 1010.22 m²
One person aged over 106.51 m²
One child under 104.64 m²
Any room under 4.64 m²Can't be used as a bedroom

Floor area under a ceiling lower than 1.5 metres doesn't count, which matters in loft rooms. Couples can share a room only if it's at least 10.22 m² and the licence allows it, and each person counts towards the house's total number of occupants.

Amenity standards

Each council publishes its own amenity standards. Typically they cover:

  • Kitchens: enough cookers, sinks, worktop, storage and fridge space for the number of people sharing
  • Bathrooms and WCs: many councils expect at least one of each for every five people
  • Heat, light and air: fixed heating in every room, natural light and good ventilation
  • Waste and common parts: enough bins, and shared areas kept clean, safe and in good repair

Always check the standards of the council where the house is. They vary, and a layout that passes in one borough can fail in the next.

Measuring a room properly

  • Measure the usable floor area, wall to wall, at floor level.
  • Exclude areas where the ceiling is below 1.5 metres.
  • Many councils exclude en-suite floor space from the bedroom figure.
  • Some councils exclude built-in cupboards or awkward corners that can't hold furniture.
  • Check the room can fit a bed, wardrobe, desk and chair with space to move.

Local standards are often higher

The national figures are the legal minimum. Many councils set higher local standards, particularly where a bedroom has no access to a shared living room. A common approach is a larger minimum for rooms in houses without a communal lounge, and an even larger one where the room includes kitchen facilities. Always read the local HMO standards document.

What happens if a room is too small

A room below the national minimum can't be used as sleeping accommodation in a licensed HMO. The council may licence the house for fewer people, require you to stop using that room as a bedroom within a set period, or take enforcement action. Under the Renters' Rights Act, civil penalties for many housing offences can now reach £40,000.

Sizes and value

Larger rooms with en-suites don't just pass inspection. They also support higher rents and stronger valuations. See are en-suites worth it? and HMO amenity standards.

Why we build above the minimum

The legal minimum is a floor, not a target. Bigger rooms let faster, keep tenants for longer and justify higher rents. We design every bedroom comfortably above the minimum, give every bedroom its own en-suite shower room, and size kitchens to the number of tenants, so the layout passes the council's standards first time.
