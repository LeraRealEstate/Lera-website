HMO mortgages: what lenders look for and how to get approved

HMO mortgages are a specialist product. Here's how they differ from buy-to-let, what lenders check and how to make your application easier.

Lera Real Estate · Updated 31 October 2026 · 3 min read

You can't usually use an ordinary buy-to-let mortgage on an HMO. Most standard buy-to-let products are written for a single tenancy, so letting rooms on separate agreements breaks their terms. Instead, you need an HMO mortgage, offered by a smaller group of specialist lenders and building societies.

The good news is that the HMO mortgage market is well established. Here's what you need to know.

How HMO mortgages differ from buy-to-let

  • The lender knows the property is let by the room, on individual tenancies or a joint tenancy.
  • Underwriting is stricter. Lenders check the property's licence, layout, room sizes and fire safety.
  • Valuations can be different. Some lenders value larger HMOs on their income, which can mean a higher valuation. See how HMOs are valued.
  • Rates are often a little higher than plain buy-to-let, reflecting the specialist nature of the lending.

What lenders typically look for

The property

  • A maximum number of bedrooms or occupants. Many lenders cap at 6 to 8 rooms. Larger HMOs need specialist or commercial lenders.
  • A valid HMO licence where one is needed, or proof you've applied.
  • Planning permission or a lawful development certificate where the house is in an Article 4 area or has 7 or more occupants.
  • Good standard of finish, fire safety and amenities.
  • A sensible location with demand for rooms.

You, the borrower

  • Experience. Many lenders want to see that you've owned or managed a rental property for a year or more. Some accept first-time HMO investors who are already landlords, and a few accept complete first-timers with a professional managing agent.
  • Personal income. Some lenders want a minimum personal income outside property, often around £25,000 a year.
  • Credit history and a clear explanation of where your deposit comes from.

The rent

Lenders check that the rent comfortably covers the mortgage interest, using a stress test. A common test is that rent must be at least 125% to 145% of the interest at a stressed rate. Limited companies often get the lower ratio, which is one reason many investors buy through a company. See buying through a limited company.

Loan to value and deposits

Most HMO mortgages lend up to 75% of the property's value, so you'll need at least a 25% deposit or equity. A few lenders go to 80% with tighter criteria. When you refinance after a conversion, the 75% is based on the new, higher value, which is how investors get much of their cash back.

Fixed or variable?

Many HMO investors choose a 5-year fixed rate for certainty, especially as rents and bills can change. Others prefer a 2-year fix or tracker to keep flexibility, for example if they plan to remortgage again once rents rise. There's no single right answer, and it's worth discussing with a specialist broker.

Common reasons applications fail

  • The property doesn't meet the lender's room size or amenity rules.
  • Missing licence or planning paperwork.
  • A valuation that comes in below expectations.
  • Rent that doesn't pass the stress test at current rates.
  • The lender's minimum ownership period hasn't passed when refinancing after works.

How to make it easier

  • Use a specialist HMO broker. They know which lenders suit which properties.
  • Have your paperwork ready: licence, planning, gas and electrical certificates, fire risk assessment, tenancy schedule and floor plans.
  • Present a clear schedule of works with costs and photos when refinancing.
  • Let the property, or show strong evidence of rents, before the valuation.
