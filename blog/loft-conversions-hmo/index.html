Loft conversions and extensions for HMOs: adding rooms the right way

Adding a bedroom in the loft or a rear extension can transform an HMO's numbers. Here's what's allowed, what it costs to get right and the traps to avoid.

Lera Real Estate · Updated 31 October 2026 · 3 min read

The most powerful way to improve an HMO's income is often to add a room. One extra en-suite bedroom can add thousands of pounds a year in rent and lift the property's value, especially where it's valued on income. Lofts and rear extensions are the usual ways to do it.

Why extra rooms matter so much

Most costs in an HMO, such as the mortgage, bills, insurance and compliance, don't rise much when you add a room. So the extra rent largely drops through to profit. On a 5-bed HMO, adding a 6th room might raise rent by a fifth while costs rise much less.

But check the size limits first. Going from 6 to 7 occupants moves an HMO from C4 to sui generis, which always needs planning permission. See HMO planning explained.

Loft conversions

Types of loft conversion

  • Velux (roof light): windows set into the existing roof slope. Cheapest, but needs good existing headroom.
  • Rear dormer: a box-shaped extension off the back roof slope. The most common choice for HMOs, as it adds lots of usable floor space.
  • Hip to gable: extends a sloping side roof into a vertical gable wall. Often combined with a dormer on semi-detached and end-terrace houses.
  • Mansard: rebuilds the roof with near-vertical walls. Gives the most space but nearly always needs planning.

Permitted development for lofts

Houses, including small C4 HMOs, often have permitted development rights for loft conversions, within limits:

  • up to 40 cubic metres of extra roof space for terraced houses
  • up to 50 cubic metres for detached and semi-detached houses
  • no extension beyond the front roof slope facing a road
  • materials similar in appearance to the existing house

Large sui generis HMOs, flats and properties in conservation areas don't have these rights. Some councils have removed them in certain areas. Get a lawful development certificate before you build.

Loft conversion checklist

  • Headroom: aim for at least 2.2 metres at the highest point of the existing roof space before you start, or plan to lower ceilings below.
  • Room size: floor area under 1.5 metres high doesn't count towards HMO minimum room sizes. Many councils apply this rule. See HMO room sizes.
  • Stairs: a proper staircase is needed, which takes space from the floor below.
  • Fire safety: a protected escape route, fire doors and linked alarms. Adding a third storey usually means upgrading the whole stair enclosure.
  • Party wall agreements with neighbours where you work on a shared wall.

Rear and side extensions

A ground-floor extension can add a bedroom, a bigger kitchen or an extra shower room. For HMOs, a larger shared kitchen and living area can be just as valuable as an extra bedroom, as councils check that shared space is adequate for the number of tenants.

Permitted development allows single-storey rear extensions within set depths, and the prior approval process can allow larger ones. Two-storey extensions usually need planning. Again, sui generis HMOs don't benefit from householder permitted development rights.

Converting other spaces

  • Garages: often converted into a ground-floor bedroom with an en-suite.
  • Front reception rooms: commonly used as bedrooms, as long as enough shared living space remains.
  • Cellars: possible, but need ceiling height, natural light, damp-proofing and a fire escape route. Usually expensive.

What does it cost?

Costs vary widely by region, size and specification. As a rough guide, a rear dormer loft conversion with an en-suite often costs several tens of thousands of pounds. Our HMO refurbishment costs guide breaks this down, and our HMO full calculator lets you test whether an extra room pays for itself.

Does the extra room pay?

A simple test: divide the cost of creating the room by the extra annual rent it brings, after bills. If a £45,000 loft room adds £8,000 a year net, it pays back in under 6 years from rent alone, before any rise in value. In many HMOs, that's one of the best returns available.
