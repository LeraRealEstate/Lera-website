What makes a great HMO? Six design choices that drive rent

Anyone can squeeze beds into a house. Great HMOs are designed around the people who live there.

Lera Real Estate · Updated 31 October 2026 · 3 min read

Anyone can squeeze beds into a house. A great HMO is designed around the people who live there. It lets quickly, keeps good tenants for longer and values well at refinance.

1. Kitchens that work

Enough cookers, sinks, worktop, storage and fridge space for everyone, so there's no queue at dinnertime. In bigger houses, two kitchens on different floors make a real difference.

2. Rooms above the minimum

The legal minimum is 6.51 m² for one person. Bigger rooms let faster, keep tenants longer and justify higher rents. See our guide to HMO room sizes.

3. An en-suite in every room

A private shower room is the single biggest driver of rent and tenant satisfaction. It also takes pressure off shared bathrooms and helps meet council amenity standards.

4. Kitchenettes

A sink and fridge in the room gives tenants extra independence and takes pressure off shared space.

5. Storage and privacy

Wardrobes, good locks, sound insulation, and a layout where no bedroom opens onto the kitchen.

6. Light, warmth and wi-fi

Natural light, efficient heating, good insulation and fast broadband in every room. Good insulation and efficient heating also help you prepare for EPC C, which rented homes will need by October 2030.

Shared spaces people actually use

A kitchen-diner with a big table, good lighting and space for everyone to cook at once does more for a house's atmosphere than almost anything else. Where space allows, a separate lounge or a garden with seating helps tenants feel at home and stay longer.

Durable, low-maintenance finishes

  • hard-wearing flooring such as quality vinyl or laminate in shared areas
  • wall panels or large tiles in showers to reduce grout cleaning
  • scrubbable paint in kitchens and hallways
  • solid, commercial-grade furniture and good mattresses

Cheap finishes look fine in photos but cost more within a few years.

Design for energy efficiency

With bills usually included, efficiency goes straight to profit. Insulation, efficient heating, smart controls and LED lighting all help, and they prepare the house for the EPC band C target. See EPC rules for HMOs and bills-included rent.

Borrowing from co-living

The best HMOs take ideas from big-city co-living: good design, fast Wi-Fi, simple pricing and responsive management, at a more affordable rent. See co-living vs HMOs.

Location matters as much as layout

The best design can't fix the wrong street. Look for strong transport links, local employers and proven room demand, in areas where the planning and licensing rules allow the HMO you want to build.
