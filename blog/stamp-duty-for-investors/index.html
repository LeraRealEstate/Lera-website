Stamp duty for property investors: rates, surcharges and reliefs

Stamp duty is often the biggest upfront cost after the deposit. Here are the current rates for investors in England, the surcharges and the reliefs that still exist.

Lera Real Estate · Updated 31 October 2026 · 3 min read

Stamp Duty Land Tax (SDLT) applies when you buy property or land in England and Northern Ireland. Scotland has Land and Buildings Transaction Tax and Wales has Land Transaction Tax, with their own rates. For investors, SDLT is often the largest cost after the deposit, so it needs to be in your numbers from the start.

This is general information. Your solicitor will confirm the exact SDLT on your purchase.

Standard residential rates

Since 1 April 2025, standard residential SDLT in England is:

Part of the priceStandard rateAdditional property rate
Up to £125,0000%5%
£125,001 to £250,0002%7%
£250,001 to £925,0005%10%
£925,001 to £1.5 million10%15%
Over £1.5 million12%17%

Like income tax, each rate only applies to the part of the price within that band.

The 5% surcharge on additional properties

If you already own a home or another residential property, or you're buying through a company, you'll normally pay the higher rates above. The surcharge rose from 3% to 5% on 31 October 2024. It applies to almost every buy-to-let and HMO purchase.

Worked example

An investor buys a house for £350,000 to convert into an HMO:

  • First £125,000 at 5% = £6,250
  • Next £125,000 at 7% = £8,750
  • Final £100,000 at 10% = £10,000
  • Total SDLT: £25,000

That's over 7% of the price, all paid in cash on completion.

Company purchases over £500,000

Companies buying a residential property over £500,000 can face a flat 17% rate on the whole price. There's a relief for genuine property rental businesses, so most investment companies pay the normal higher rates instead, but they must claim the relief correctly and keep the property let to unconnected people. If a company stops qualifying within three years, the higher charge can come back.

Non-UK residents

Buyers who aren't UK resident pay a further 2% surcharge on top of the rates above. The residence test for SDLT is different from the one for income tax, so check carefully if you've spent time abroad.

Reliefs that have gone

Multiple Dwellings Relief was abolished for purchases completing from 1 June 2024. It used to cut SDLT when buying several dwellings in one go, such as a house with an annex or a block of flats. Plan without it.

Reliefs and rules that still matter

  • Six or more dwellings bought in a single transaction can be charged at non-residential rates, which top out at 5%.
  • Mixed-use property, such as a shop with a flat above, is charged at non-residential rates.
  • Uninhabitable property may be treated as non-residential in rare cases, but the test is strict and HMRC regularly challenges claims. A tired house that needs a new kitchen does not qualify.
  • Replacing your main home: if you sell your old main home within three years, you may claim back the surcharge. This doesn't apply to investment purchases.

Is an HMO one dwelling or several?

A typical shared house with a shared kitchen is treated as one dwelling. A building split into self-contained flats, each with its own kitchen and bathroom, may be treated as several dwellings. This affects which rates apply, so get advice before you buy anything unusual.

Paying and planning

  • SDLT is due within 14 days of completion. Your solicitor normally files the return and pays it from funds you provide.
  • It counts as part of the purchase cost, so it isn't deductible against rent, but it is added to the base cost when you calculate capital gains tax on a future sale.
  • Bridging and mortgage lenders don't usually lend against SDLT, so it comes from your own cash.
