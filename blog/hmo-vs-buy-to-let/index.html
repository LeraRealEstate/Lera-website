HMO vs buy-to-let: why the same house can earn 2.4× the rent

Letting room by room turns an ordinary family home into a high-yield investment. Here's why, and what it costs.

Lera Real Estate · Updated 31 October 2026 · 3 min read

Take one ordinary London house and let it two different ways. As a single family let, our Greenford UB6 house would rent for around £2,500 a month. Converted into six en-suite rooms, its independent valuation put the rent at around £6,000 a month. Same house, about 2.4 times the rent.

Why rooms earn more

London's average private rent is £2,332 a month (ONS, August 2026), while the average London room rents for £932 (SpareRoom, Q3 2026). Each tenant pays far less than they would for a whole home, but six rooms together add up to much more than one family rent for the same house.

One void, not zero rent

In a single let, an empty house earns nothing. In a six-room HMO, if one room is empty the other five keep paying. Spreading income across several tenants makes the rent far more resilient.

The costs are higher too

An honest comparison has to include the extra running costs. In an HMO the landlord usually pays:

  • gas, electricity, water and broadband (rooms are let bills-included)
  • council tax
  • cleaning of shared areas
  • furniture, a licence, extra fire safety and more compliance checks
  • more intensive management

Every return we quote is calculated after all of these, and after our fees.

The tax picture

Tax affects both strategies, but not equally. Individual landlords can't deduct mortgage interest in full and instead get a 20% tax credit, so higher-rate taxpayers often pay tax on money spent on interest. An HMO's higher rent means interest is a smaller share of income, so it copes better. Many investors buy through a limited company for this reason. Time and management

A single let can be very hands-off once a good tenant is in. An HMO has more tenants, more turnover and more compliance, so it needs professional management or real time from you. That difference is why done-for-you models exist. See managing an HMO.

Value you create

There's a second advantage. Converting a house adds rooms, en-suites and income, and lenders value HMOs partly on what they earn. A good conversion can be worth far more than it cost, and that extra value can be released through a refinance.
